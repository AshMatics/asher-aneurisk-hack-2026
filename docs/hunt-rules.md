# The spot-the-flaw 'hunt'

## The setup

- **Five practice hospitals**, released Friday night, each in a folder with the same structure (see [data-card.md](data-card.md)).
- Each hospital hides **one or two planted problems** ("Waldos"). Some are about who the patients are, some about how they were scanned, some about how they were labeled, some about how the data was assembled.
- **At least one hospital is clean.** Saying so, with evidence, is a correct answer.
- **Two tutorial answer keys** are released Friday night so you can calibrate your tool.
- **All practice answer keys** are released Saturday evening, with reference measures for each hospital. See what you missed and tune.
- **Two more hospitals** arrive Sunday morning. No keys. This is the real test.

## Posting a find

Post finds to the Waldo board (link at kickoff). Each find names:

1. The hospital
2. The **kind** of problem (for example: label prior, acquisition or scanner, labeling convention, population, clustering or duplication, shortcut)
3. Severity, if the hospital has more than one problem or you are comparing hospitals
4. One or two sentences of evidence a clinician could follow

## Feedback

The sponsors play the three customers all weekend. They are not the event judges. Nucleate's judges assess the final projects and award all prizes. The customers give you practice feedback before judging:

- **Your chosen customer** tells you whether they would use what you built, and what is missing.
- **The answer keys** tell you which flaws you found, which you missed, and where you raised a false alarm.

**False alarms matter.** Calling a clean hospital broken is as misleading as missing a real flaw. "This hospital is clean, and here is the evidence" is a correct answer.

**Naming the kind of problem matters.** "Hospital 4 is different" tells a customer little. "Hospital 4 measures aneurysms differently, and here is how we know" tells them what to do.

## Spirit of the thing

The strongest submissions tend to find something unflattering and report it honestly, explain results so a non-ML reader can act on them, and can be reproduced by someone else quickly.
